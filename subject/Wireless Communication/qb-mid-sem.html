THEORY · Q1

OFDM transmitter aur receiver — block diagram

QUESTION

Draw and explain the complete block diagram of an OFDM transmitter and receiver. Explain the function of each block.

ANSWER

OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) mein tez data stream ko N slow parallel subcarriers par baant diya jaata hai. Subcarriers orthogonal hote hain — spacing Δf = 1/Tu (Tu = useful symbol time) — isliye overlap hone ke baad bhi aapas mein interfere nahi karte. Slow symbols + cyclic prefix milkar multipath ISI ko khatm karte hain. Yehi OFDM 4G/5G aur Wi-Fi ki jaan hai.

EXAM DEFINITION

OFDM is a multicarrier modulation technique in which high-rate data is split across many orthogonal low-rate subcarriers, making the system robust against multipath fading and ISI.

Transmitter chain (left se right, har block ka function):

  • Serial-to-Parallel (S/P): tez serial bits ko N slow streams mein baantta hai — symbol time N guna badh jaata hai, ISI se ladna easy hota hai.
  • Modulation mapper: bits ko constellation points par map karta hai (QPSK/16-QAM/64-QAM — Q2 dekho). Har subcarrier par ek complex symbol baithta hai.
  • IFFT block: saare subcarrier symbols ko ek saath time-domain signal mein badalta hai — yahi OFDM modulation ka dil hai (MCQ 1: FFT/IFFT).
  • Cyclic prefix (CP) insertion: har symbol ke end ka tukda shuru mein chipkata hai — multipath ke late echoes ISI nahi banate (MCQ 2). Keemat: thodi efficiency ghatti hai (Q4).
  • Parallel-to-Serial + DAC + RF up-conversion: digital samples ko analog banakar carrier frequency par chadhata hai aur antenna se bhejta hai.

Receiver chain (exact ulta): antenna → RF down-conversion → ADC → S/P → CP remove → FFT (subcarriers wapas alag) → demapper (symbols se bits) → P/S (original data). FFT window sahi jagah lagna chahiye — isliye synchronization chahiye (Q5).

Exam-ready plan: definition + orthogonality line (Δf = 1/Tu), Tx ke 5 blocks har ek ek-line function ke saath, Rx ulta chain, FIG.001, end mein CP = ISI kavach aur IFFT/FFT = dil wali line. Diagram ke bina aadhe marks gaye.

Analogy: OFDM highway par ek truck ki jagah kai chhoti gaadiyan bhejna hai — har gaadi slow par safe, aur gaadiyon ke beech itna gap (orthogonality) ki takrayein nahi.

THEORY · Q2

OFDM ke liye modulation scheme chunne ke parameters

QUESTION

Explain the important parameters involved in selecting a modulation scheme for an OFDM system.

ANSWER

Modulation scheme chunna speed vs majbooti ka samjhota hai: high-order modulation (64-QAM) zyada bits bhejti hai par shor mein jaldi toot-ti hai; low-order (QPSK) slow par pathhar jaisi majboot hai. Exam mein 6–7 parameters headings mein likho:

  • Data rate / throughput: kitne bits/symbol — BPSK = 1, QPSK = 2, 16-QAM = 4, 64-QAM = 6. Zyada bits = zyada speed.
  • BER robustness (noise se ladna): constellation points jitne paas, shor mein utni galti. Kharab channel mein QPSK (MCQ 3), achhe channel mein 64-QAM.
  • Required SNR: har scheme ko target BER ke liye minimum SNR chahiye — high-order ko zyada SNR maangta hai.
  • Bandwidth / spectral efficiency: zyada bits/symbol = kam bandwidth mein zyada data (Q4 se jodo).
  • PAPR sensitivity: OFDM mein high peaks hote hain; high-order QAM amplifier non-linearity se zyada bigadta hai.
  • Complexity aur power: mobile battery + sasta hardware chaahe toh simple scheme (QPSK) behtar.
  • Mobility / Doppler: tez chalti gaadi/train mein channel jaldi badalta hai — robust low-order scheme safe rehta hai.

Golden line (adaptive modulation): modern systems (LTE/5G) channel dekhkar scheme badalte rehte hain — paas ho toh 64-QAM, door ho toh QPSK. Is ek line se answer topper jaisa lagta hai.

THEORY · Q3

Pulse shaping kya hai? Zaroorat kyun?

QUESTION

What is pulse shaping? Explain the need for pulse shaping in digital communication systems.

ANSWER

Pulse shaping matlab digital 0/1 ke tez-dhaar (rectangular) pulses ko gol-mol (smooth) shape dena taaki wo kam bandwidth lein aur padosi symbols mein na ghusein. Bina shaping ke do museebatein aati hain:

  • Infinite bandwidth: rectangular pulse ke tez kinare frequency mein door tak failte hain — spectrum kharab, padosi channel mein ghuspaith. Shaped pulse ka spectrum simta rehta hai.
  • ISI (inter-symbol interference): channel aur multipath pulse ko faila dete hain; ek symbol ki poonchh agle symbol par chadh jaati hai. Shaping + Nyquist condition se har sampling instant par padosi pulses zero hote hain — ISI khatm.
EXAM DEFINITION

Pulse shaping is the process of modifying transmitted pulses to limit their bandwidth and achieve zero ISI at sampling instants (Nyquist criterion).

Raised-cosine filter (MCQ 4 ka answer): sabse standard pulse-shaping filter. Iska roll-off factor α (0 se 1) samjhota control karta hai — α = 0 matlab minimum bandwidth par sensitive timing, α badhao toh bandwidth thoda badhta hai par timing errors se ladna easy hota hai. Practical value α ≈ 0.22–0.35 (GSM mein use hota hai).

Exam-ready plan: definition, 2 zarooratein (bandwidth + ISI with Nyquist line), raised-cosine + α tradeoff, windowing ek line (MCQ 5: transmitted signal ke spectral characteristics sudharta hai), FIG.002.

THEORY · Q4

Spectral efficiency — definition, derivation, factors

QUESTION

Define spectral efficiency. Derive the expression for spectral efficiency of an OFDM system and explain the factors affecting it.

ANSWER

Spectral efficiency (η) batati hai ki 1 Hz bandwidth se kitne bits/sec nikaale — unit bits/s/Hz. Formula: η = Rb / B (Rb = bit rate, B = bandwidth). Spectrum sarkari khazana hai (limited + costly), isliye η jitni zyada, operator utna khush (MCQ 6).

EXAM DEFINITION

Spectral efficiency indicates how efficiently the available bandwidth is used to transmit information: η = data rate / bandwidth (bits/s/Hz).

Derivation (5 steps, exam mein yahi likho):

  • Step 1: N subcarriers, har symbol par M-ary modulation → har subcarrier log2(M) bits. Ek OFDM symbol mein kul bits = N · log2(M).
  • Step 2: Symbol time = useful time + CP: Ts = Tu + Tcp. Bit rate Rb = N · log2(M) / (Tu + Tcp).
  • Step 3: Orthogonality se subcarrier spacing Δf = 1/Tu, kul bandwidth B ≈ N · Δf = N / Tu.
  • Step 4: η = Rb / B = [N·log2(M)/(Tu+Tcp)] / [N/Tu] = log2(M) · Tu/(Tu+Tcp).
  • Step 5 (final): CP fraction G = Tcp/Tu rakho → η = log2(M) / (1 + G). Bina CP (G = 0) → η = log2(M).

Numeric example (pakka yaad karo): 64-QAM → log2(64) = 6 bits; CP = 1/4 (G = 0.25) → η = 6/1.25 = 4.8 bits/s/Hz. Wahi system QPSK mein hota toh η = 2/1.25 = 1.6 — isi se Q2 ka speed-vs-majbooti tradeoff judta hai.

Factors affecting (5 points): modulation order M (bada M = zyada η, kam majbooti), CP length (lamba CP = ISI se suraksha par η ghategi), guard bands/pilots ka overhead, channel coding rate (error correction ke bits η ghataate hain), windowing/filtering (side-lobes kam → effective η better).

Exam-ready plan: definition + formula + unit, 5-step derivation box mein, numeric example (4.8 wali), 5 factors. Derivation steps numbering ke saath likho — step-marks milte hain.

OFDM mein timing aur frequency synchronization

QUESTION

Explain timing synchronization and frequency synchronization in OFDM.

ANSWER

OFDM sync-sensitive hai: FFT window galat jagah lagi toh ISI, aur carrier frequency thodi bhi hili toh orthogonality tootkar ICI (inter-carrier interference) — padosi subcarriers aapas mein ladne lagte hain. Isliye receiver ko do cheezein lock karni padti hain:

THEORY · Q6

Cellular mein channel assignment strategies

QUESTION

Explain channel assignment strategies in cellular systems.

ANSWER

Limited channels ko cells mein baantne ke 3 tareeke hain. Socho shaadi mein khana: fixed thaali (FCA), buffet line (DCA), mix (HCA).

  • FCA (Fixed Channel Assignment): har cell ko fixed channels — simple, par rush hour mein ek cell full aur padosi khaali (trunking loss). Channel borrowing: busy cell padosi se udhaar le sakta hai (interference check ke saath).
  • DCA (Dynamic Channel Assignment): koi fixed kota nahi — har call par MSC interference dekhkar channel deta hai. Centralized (MSC decide) ya distributed (BTS decide). Flexible + capacity zyada, par signalling/complexity zyada.
  • HCA (Hybrid): kuch fixed + kuch dynamic pool — dono ke fayde; zyada load par dynamic pool sambhalta hai.

THEORY · Q7

Frequency reuse, interference aur capacity ka rishta

QUESTION

Explain the relationship between frequency reuse, interference and capacity.

ANSWER

Ye cellular ka golden triangle hai — ek badlo, baaki do hilte hain:

  • Frequency reuse: limited spectrum ko door-door ke cells mein dobara use karna. Cluster size N (jaise N = 7) — N cells milkar poora spectrum ek baar use karte hain; reuse factor = 1/N. N chhota → frequency paas-paas reuse → capacity zyada.
  • Interference (co-channel): same frequency wale door ke cells ek-dusre mein shor daalte hain — S/I ratio (signal-to-interference, MCQ 15) quality batata hai. Cells paas → S/I kharab → call drop.
  • Capacity: area mein kul calls/channels. N chhota → har cell ko zyada channels → capacity zyada, par interference bhi zyada. N bada → S/I achha, par capacity kam.
GOLDEN TRADEOFF

Small N → high capacity + high interference (S/I low). Large N → low interference (S/I high) + low capacity. Cell splitting (MCQ 13) capacity badhane ka tareeka hai: bade cell ko chhote cells mein todo, transmit power घटाओ, reuse badhao.

Exam-ready plan: teeno definitions, tradeoff dono direction mein (small N vs large N), S/I line, cell splitting + handoff (MCQ 14: continuity) + GoS (MCQ 17) ek line, FIG.004.

Analogy: Mohalle mein ek hi park (spectrum) — paas-paas colony mein baanto toh sabko milta hai (capacity) par bheed-shor badhta hai (interference); door-door baanto toh shaanti par kam logon ko milta hai.

THEORY · Q8

GPRS — architecture, features, working

QUESTION

What is GPRS? Explain its architecture, features, and working in detail.

ANSWER

GPRS (General Packet Radio Service) GSM par packet-data ki suvidha hai — 2.5G. Circuit-switched call (poori line book) ki jagah data packets mein jaata hai, isliye always-on + pay-per-data (kitna data, utna paisa — time ka nahi).

EXAM DEFINITION

GPRS is a packet-switched data service over GSM networks that enables always-on mobile internet charged by data volume rather than connection time.

Architecture — GSM + 3 naye dost (FIG.005):

  • PCU (Packet Control Unit, BSC mein): radio packets ko circuit voice se alag karta hai.
  • SGSN (Serving GPRS Support Node): apne area ke users ka mobility + routing sambhalta hai (packet wala MSC samjho).
  • GGSN (Gateway GSN): internet/duniya ka darwaza — packets bahar bhejta/laata hai.
  • Baaqi GSM same: MS → BTS → BSC → (voice MSC taraf, data SGSN taraf) → HLR/VLR record.

Features: always-on connection, volume-based billing, ~171.2 kbps peak (8 time-slots × CS-4), 4 coding schemes CS-1 se CS-4 (signal achha → CS-4 fast, kharab → CS-1 safe — Q2 wala tradeoff!), SMS + internet ek saath, existing GSM par upgrade (naya tower nahi).

Working (4 steps): 1) GPRS attach — mobile SGSN se judta hai. 2) PDP context activate — IP address milta hai. 3) Data packets BTS→PCU→SGSN→GGSN→internet. 4) Idle par radio free, logical connection bana rehta hai (isliye always-on sasta hai).

Exam-ready plan: definition + 2.5G line, 3 naye nodes har ek ek-line function, 4–5 features (always-on, billing, speed, CS-1–4), 4-step working, FIG.005.

THEORY · Q9

GSM architecture — complete diagram

QUESTION

Draw and explain the complete GSM architecture.

ANSWER

GSM (Global System for Mobile communications) 2G digital cellular standard hai — voice + SMS + (GPRS se) data. Poora network 3 hisson mein: mobile, radio access, core.

Blocks aur functions (FIG.006):

  • MS (Mobile Station = phone + SIM): user ka device; SIM identity (IMSI) deta hai.
  • BTS (Base Transceiver Station = tower): radio se phone se baat karta hai; BCCH par system info broadcast karta hai (MCQ 10), TCH par voice (full-rate = 1 slot/frame, MCQ 11), urgent signalling par FACCH (MCQ 12). Mobile↔network ka pul (MCQ 9).
  • BSC (Base Station Controller): kai BTS sambhalta hai — handoff decisions, frequency hopping.
  • MSC (Mobile Switching Centre): call switching ka head-office; PSTN se jodta hai.
  • HLR/VLR (registers): HLR permanent record (tum kaun + plan), VLR visiting-area copy. EIR chori ke phone (IMEI) rokta hai, AuC authentication karta hai.
  • Interfaces: MS↔BTS = Um (air), BTS↔BSC = Abis, BSC↔MSC = A — ye teeno naam exam mein number dilate hain.

Speech coding (MCQ 8): traditional GSM ≈ 13 kbps (RPE-LTP coder) — voice ko itna compress karke bheja jaata hai.

Exam-ready plan: 3 hisson wali line, 6 blocks har ek function + MCQ link (BCCH/TCH/FACCH/BTS/13 kbps), 3 interface naam, FIG.006. Diagram mein HLR/VLR box zaroor banao.

THEORY · Q11

Cellular communication mein current trends

QUESTION

Explain the current trends in cellular communication systems.

ANSWER

Cellular ab sirf “call” nahi — speed + capacity + smart services ki race hai. 8 trends, har ek ek-line matlab ke saath:

  • 5G → 6G: 5G (Gbps, 1 ms latency) roll-out, 6G research (Tbps soch, AI-native network, 2030 target).
  • Massive MIMO: tower par 64+ antennas — ek saath kai users ko beamform karke capacity multifold.
  • mmWave + small cells: 24+ GHz par bahut bandwidth; range kam toh gali-gali chhote cells (het-nets) — Q7 ke cell splitting ka modern roop.
  • Network slicing: ek physical network ke kai virtual tukde — car ke liye ultra-reliable slice, video ke liye high-speed slice.
  • Edge computing (MEC): data center tower ke paas — gaming/AR ka response instant.
  • Massive IoT (mMTC): ek cell mein laakhon sensors (smart city, agriculture) — kam data, lambi battery.
  • Open RAN: khula multi-vendor radio standard — operator saste mein mix-match hardware lagaye.
  • AI-driven networks: traffic prediction, self-healing, energy saving — network khud optimize kare.

Exam-ready plan: race wali opening line, 8 trends bullets, har bullet mein ek example, end mein Q10 se link (5G row) — poora assignment ek kahani lagega.

  1. Q1. The commonly used mathematical transform for OFDM generation is?

  3. Q3. Which modulation is generally more robust against noise?

    A) 64-QAM · B) 256-QAM · C) QPSK · D) 1024-QAM
    Answer: C — points door-door = shor se suraksha; order badhe toh najakat badhe (Q2 table).

  4. Q4. Commonly used pulse-shaping filter is?

    A) Raised-cosine filter · B) Low-pass DC filter · C) Battery filter · D) Optical filter

  5. Q5. Windowing can improve the?

    A) Spectral characteristics of the transmitted signal · B) Battery capacity · C) Antenna height · D) Number of bits in a symbol
    Answer: A — side-lobes dabte hain, spectrum saaf hota hai (Q3).

  6. Q6. Spectral efficiency indicates?

    A) How efficiently bandwidth is used to transmit information · B) Transmitter physical size · C) Antenna height · D) Battery life
    Answer: A — η = Rb/B bits/s/Hz (Q4).

  7. Q7. Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO) can cause?

    A) Inter-carrier interference · B) Increased battery capacity · C) Reduced sampling · D) Elimination of noise
    Answer: A — frequency hili → orthogonality tooti → ICI (Q5).

  8. Q8. The standard speech coding rate commonly associated with traditional GSM is approximately?

    A) 64 kbps · B) 13 kbps · C) 128 kbps · D) 2 Mbps
    Answer: B — GSM full-rate voice ≈ 13 kbps (Q9).

  9. Q9. BTS provides communication between?

    A) Mobile station and network · B) HLR and VLR · C) MSC and HLR only · D) Two HLRs
    Answer: A — tower hi phone ko network se jodta hai (Q9, FIG.006).

  10. Q10. Which channel broadcasts system information to mobile stations?

    A) BCCH · B) TCH · C) SDCCH · D) FACCH
    Answer: A — BCCH = broadcast; TCH voice, FACCH urgent signalling (Q9).

  11. Q11. A full-rate GSM traffic channel occupies?

    A) One time slot per frame · B) Two time slots per frame · C) Four time slots per frame · D) Eight time slots per frame
    Answer: A — full-rate = 1 slot, half-rate = aadha slot (Q9).

  12. Q12. FACCH is used when?

    A) Urgent signalling is required · B) No signalling is required · C) Only SMS is transmitted · D) Only frequency correction is required
    Answer: A — FACCH voice slot chheenkar emergency signalling bhejta hai (Q9).

  13. Q13. Which technique helps increase cellular network capacity by serving users with smaller coverage areas?

    A) Cell splitting · B) Frequency elimination · C) Modulation removal · D) Trunking reduction
    Answer: A — chhote cells = zyada reuse = zyada capacity (Q7).

  14. Q14. The main objective of handoff is to?

    A) Maintain call/service continuity · B) Reduce the number of cells · C) Increase mobile size · D) Stop communication
    Answer: A — chalte call ko tower-to-tower saunpna (Q6/Q7).

  15. Q15. The signal-to-interference ratio is commonly represented by?

    A) S/I · B) I/S · C) P/T · D) R/N
    Answer: A — signal upar, interference neeche; S/I bada = quality achhi (Q7).

  16. Q16. Which technique can improve coverage in areas with weak signal strength?

    A) Repeaters · B) Frequency elimination · C) Reducing antenna height to zero · D) Removing base stations
    Answer: A — repeater signal pakadkar aage badhata hai; C/D options ulta kaam karte hain.

  17. Q17. Grade of Service (GoS) indicates?

    A) Probability that a call is blocked or delayed · B) Battery quality · C) Antenna height · D) Data encryption level
    Answer: A — GoS = blocking probability (Erlang), network dimensioning ka meter (Q7).

THEORY · Q1

OFDM transmitter and receiver — block diagram

QUESTION

Draw and explain the complete block diagram of an OFDM transmitter and receiver. Explain the function of each block.

ANSWER

In OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing), a fast data stream is split across N slow parallel subcarriers. Subcarriers are orthogonal — spaced Δf = 1/Tu (Tu = useful symbol time) — so they overlap yet never interfere. Slow symbols plus the cyclic prefix kill multipath ISI. This is the heart of 4G/5G and Wi-Fi.

EXAM DEFINITION

OFDM is a multicarrier modulation technique in which high-rate data is split across many orthogonal low-rate subcarriers, making the system robust against multipath fading and ISI.

Transmitter chain (left to right, each block's function):

  • Serial-to-Parallel (S/P): splits fast serial bits into N slow streams — symbol time grows N times, fighting ISI becomes easy.
  • Modulation mapper: maps bits onto constellation points (QPSK/16-QAM/64-QAM — see Q2). One complex symbol sits on each subcarrier.
  • IFFT block: converts all subcarrier symbols into one time-domain signal — the heart of OFDM modulation (MCQ 1: FFT/IFFT).
  • Cyclic prefix (CP) insertion: pastes each symbol's tail at its start — late multipath echoes cause no ISI (MCQ 2). Price: slightly lower efficiency (Q4).
  • Parallel-to-Serial + DAC + RF up-conversion: converts digital samples to analog, mounts them on the carrier, and transmits via antenna.

Receiver chain (exact reverse): antenna → RF down-conversion → ADC → S/P → CP removal → FFT (subcarriers separated again) → demapper (symbols to bits) → P/S (original data). The FFT window must sit correctly — hence synchronization (Q5).

Exam-ready plan: definition + orthogonality line (Δf = 1/Tu), 5 Tx blocks each with a one-line function, reverse Rx chain, FIG.001, ending with the CP = ISI shield and IFFT/FFT = heart lines. Without the diagram, half the marks are gone.

THEORY · Q2

Parameters for selecting an OFDM modulation scheme

QUESTION

Explain the important parameters involved in selecting a modulation scheme for an OFDM system.

ANSWER

Choosing modulation is a speed vs toughness deal: high-order modulation (64-QAM) sends more bits but breaks easily in noise; low-order (QPSK) is slow but rock-solid. Write 6–7 parameters under headings in the exam:

  • Data rate / throughput: bits per symbol — BPSK = 1, QPSK = 2, 16-QAM = 4, 64-QAM = 6. More bits = more speed.
  • BER robustness: closer constellation points mean more errors in noise. QPSK for bad channels (MCQ 3), 64-QAM for good ones.
  • Required SNR: each scheme needs a minimum SNR for the target BER — high-order demands more SNR.
  • Bandwidth / spectral efficiency: more bits per symbol = more data in less bandwidth (link to Q4).
  • PAPR sensitivity: OFDM has high peaks; high-order QAM suffers more from amplifier non-linearity.
  • Complexity and power: mobile batteries and cheap hardware prefer simple schemes (QPSK).
  • Mobility / Doppler: in fast cars/trains the channel changes quickly — robust low-order schemes stay safe.

Golden line (adaptive modulation): modern systems (LTE/5G) keep switching schemes by channel — 64-QAM near, QPSK far. This one line makes the answer look topper-class.

THEORY · Q3

What is pulse shaping? Why is it needed?

QUESTION

What is pulse shaping? Explain the need for pulse shaping in digital communication systems.

ANSWER

Pulse shaping means rounding the sharp rectangular pulses of digital 0/1 into smooth shapes so they occupy less bandwidth and never leak into neighbouring symbols. Without shaping, two troubles come:

  • Infinite bandwidth: sharp pulse edges spread far in frequency — bad spectrum, intrusion into neighbour channels. Shaped pulses stay compact.
  • ISI (inter-symbol interference): channel and multipath smear pulses; one symbol's tail climbs onto the next. With shaping + Nyquist condition, neighbouring pulses are zero at every sampling instant — ISI gone.
EXAM DEFINITION

Pulse shaping is the process of modifying transmitted pulses to limit their bandwidth and achieve zero ISI at sampling instants (Nyquist criterion).

Raised-cosine filter (answer to MCQ 4): the standard pulse-shaping filter. Its roll-off factor α (0 to 1) controls the deal — α = 0 means minimum bandwidth but sensitive timing; larger α costs a little bandwidth but fights timing errors easily. Practical α ≈ 0.22–0.35 (used in GSM).

Exam-ready plan: definition, 2 needs (bandwidth + ISI with Nyquist line), raised-cosine + α tradeoff, windowing one-liner (MCQ 5: improves spectral characteristics of the transmitted signal), FIG.002.

THEORY · Q4

Spectral efficiency — definition, derivation, factors

QUESTION

Define spectral efficiency. Derive the expression for spectral efficiency of an OFDM system and explain the factors affecting it.

ANSWER

Spectral efficiency (η) tells how many bits/sec are squeezed from 1 Hz of bandwidth — unit bits/s/Hz. Formula: η = Rb / B (Rb = bit rate, B = bandwidth). Spectrum is government treasure (limited + costly), so higher η means a happier operator (MCQ 6).

EXAM DEFINITION

Spectral efficiency indicates how efficiently the available bandwidth is used to transmit information: η = data rate / bandwidth (bits/s/Hz).

Derivation (write exactly these 5 steps in the exam):

  • Step 1: N subcarriers with M-ary modulation → log2(M) bits each. Total bits per OFDM symbol = N · log2(M).
  • Step 2: Symbol time = useful time + CP: Ts = Tu + Tcp. Bit rate Rb = N · log2(M) / (Tu + Tcp).
  • Step 3: Orthogonality gives subcarrier spacing Δf = 1/Tu, total bandwidth B ≈ N · Δf = N / Tu.
  • Step 4: η = Rb / B = [N·log2(M)/(Tu+Tcp)] / [N/Tu] = log2(M) · Tu/(Tu+Tcp).
  • Step 5 (final): with CP fraction G = Tcp/Tu → η = log2(M) / (1 + G). Without CP (G = 0) → η = log2(M).

Numeric example (memorize firmly): 64-QAM → log2(64) = 6 bits; CP = 1/4 (G = 0.25) → η = 6/1.25 = 4.8 bits/s/Hz. The same system in QPSK would give η = 2/1.25 = 1.6 — this links to the Q2 speed-vs-toughness tradeoff.

Factors affecting (5 points): modulation order M (bigger M = higher η, lower toughness), CP length (long CP = ISI protection but lower η), guard-band/pilot overhead, channel coding rate (error-correction bits lower η), windowing/filtering (fewer side-lobes → better effective η).

Exam-ready plan: definition + formula + unit, 5-step derivation in a numbered box, numeric example (the 4.8 one), 5 factors. Numbered derivation steps earn step-marks.

THEORY · Q5

Timing and frequency synchronization in OFDM

QUESTION

Explain timing synchronization and frequency synchronization in OFDM.

ANSWER

OFDM is sync-sensitive: a misplaced FFT window causes ISI, and even a small carrier-frequency shift breaks orthogonality into ICI (inter-carrier interference) — neighbouring subcarriers start fighting. So the receiver must lock two things:

PointTiming syncFrequency sync
What locksSymbol start (FFT window position)Carrier frequency (CFO = 0)
Error causesISI — if window leaves the CPICI — orthogonality breaks (MCQ 7)

Timing sync detail: the receiver needs every symbol's start at sample level; while the window stays inside the CP there is no ISI (the CP absorbs exactly this). Packets carry a preamble (known pattern) at the start — the Schmidl–Cox method correlates it to catch the symbol boundary.

Frequency sync detail: CFO (carrier frequency offset) comes from transmitter-receiver oscillator difference and Doppler; even a small CFO shifts subcarriers into ICI (MCQ 7's answer: inter-carrier interference). CFO is estimated from pilot tones and fixed by AFC (automatic frequency control).

THEORY · Q6

Channel assignment strategies in cellular systems

QUESTION

Explain channel assignment strategies in cellular systems.

ANSWER

Three ways to share limited channels among cells. Think of a wedding dinner: fixed plates (FCA), buffet line (DCA), mix (HCA).

  • FCA (Fixed Channel Assignment): fixed channels per cell — simple, but at rush hour one cell is full while the neighbour sits empty (trunking loss). Channel borrowing: a busy cell may borrow from a neighbour (with interference checks).
  • DCA (Dynamic Channel Assignment): no fixed quota — the MSC assigns a channel per call after checking interference. Centralized (MSC decides) or distributed (BTS decides). More flexible + more capacity, but more signalling/complexity.
  • HCA (Hybrid): some fixed + some dynamic pool — benefits of both; the dynamic pool absorbs heavy load.

THEORY · Q7

Relationship between frequency reuse, interference and capacity

QUESTION

Explain the relationship between frequency reuse, interference and capacity.

ANSWER

This is cellular's golden triangle — move one corner and the other two shift:

  • Frequency reuse: reusing limited spectrum in far-apart cells. Cluster size N (e.g. N = 7) — N cells together use the full spectrum once; reuse factor = 1/N. Small N → close reuse → higher capacity.
  • Interference (co-channel): distant same-frequency cells inject noise into each other — the S/I ratio (signal-to-interference, MCQ 15) reports quality. Close cells → bad S/I → dropped calls.
  • Capacity: total calls/channels in an area. Small N → more channels per cell → higher capacity, but also higher interference. Large N → good S/I, but lower capacity.
GOLDEN TRADEOFF

Small N → high capacity + high interference (low S/I). Large N → low interference (high S/I) + low capacity. Cell splitting (MCQ 13) raises capacity: split big cells into small ones, lower transmit power, raise reuse.

Exam-ready plan: all three definitions, tradeoff in both directions (small N vs large N), S/I line, one line each on cell splitting + handoff (MCQ 14: continuity) + GoS (MCQ 17), FIG.004.

THEORY · Q8

GPRS — architecture, features, working

QUESTION

What is GPRS? Explain its architecture, features, and working in detail.

ANSWER

GPRS (General Packet Radio Service) is packet data over GSM — 2.5G. Instead of booking a full circuit-switched line per call, data travels in packets, giving always-on + pay-per-data (pay for volume, not time).

EXAM DEFINITION

GPRS is a packet-switched data service over GSM networks that enables always-on mobile internet charged by data volume rather than connection time.

Architecture — GSM + 3 new friends (FIG.005):

  • PCU (Packet Control Unit, in BSC): separates radio packets from circuit voice.
  • SGSN (Serving GPRS Support Node): handles mobility + routing for users in its area (think packet-side MSC).
  • GGSN (Gateway GSN): the door to the internet — sends/fetches packets outside.
  • Rest same as GSM: MS → BTS → BSC → (voice toward MSC, data toward SGSN) → HLR/VLR records.

Features: always-on connection, volume-based billing, ~171.2 kbps peak (8 time-slots × CS-4), 4 coding schemes CS-1 to CS-4 (good signal → fast CS-4, bad → safe CS-1 — the Q2 tradeoff again!), SMS + internet together, upgrade over existing GSM (no new towers).

Working (4 steps): 1) GPRS attach — mobile joins the SGSN. 2) PDP context activation — IP address granted. 3) Data packets flow BTS→PCU→SGSN→GGSN→internet. 4) When idle the radio frees up while the logical connection persists (why always-on stays cheap).

Exam-ready plan: definition + 2.5G line, 3 new nodes each with a one-line function, 4–5 features (always-on, billing, speed, CS-1–4), 4-step working, FIG.005.

THEORY · Q9

GSM architecture — complete diagram

QUESTION

Draw and explain the complete GSM architecture.

ANSWER

GSM (Global System for Mobile communications) is the 2G digital cellular standard — voice + SMS + (via GPRS) data. The whole network has 3 parts: mobile, radio access, core.

Blocks and functions (FIG.006):

  • MS (Mobile Station = phone + SIM): the user's device; the SIM gives identity (IMSI).
  • BTS (Base Transceiver Station = tower): talks radio to phones; broadcasts system info on BCCH (MCQ 10), carries voice on TCH (full-rate = 1 slot/frame, MCQ 11), urgent signalling on FACCH (MCQ 12). The bridge between mobile and network (MCQ 9).
  • BSC (Base Station Controller): manages several BTS units — handoff decisions, frequency hopping.
  • MSC (Mobile Switching Centre): the call-switching head office; connects to PSTN.
  • HLR/VLR (registers): HLR is the permanent record (who you are + plan), VLR a visiting-area copy. EIR blocks stolen phones (IMEI), AuC authenticates.
  • Interfaces: MS↔BTS = Um (air), BTS↔BSC = Abis, BSC↔MSC = A — these three names earn marks in the exam.

Speech coding (MCQ 8): traditional GSM ≈ 13 kbps (RPE-LTP coder) — voice is compressed this far before sending.

Exam-ready plan: 3-parts line, 6 blocks each with function + MCQ link (BCCH/TCH/FACCH/BTS/13 kbps), 3 interface names, FIG.006. Always draw the HLR/VLR box in the diagram.

THEORY · Q11

Current trends in cellular communication

QUESTION

Explain the current trends in cellular communication systems.

ANSWER

Cellular is no longer just “calls” — it is a race of speed + capacity + smart services. 8 trends, each with a one-line meaning:

  • 5G → 6G: 5G rolling out (Gbps, 1 ms latency), 6G research (Tbps dreams, AI-native networks, 2030 target).
  • Massive MIMO: 64+ antennas on towers — beamforming to many users at once, multifold capacity.
  • mmWave + small cells: huge bandwidth above 24 GHz; short range means street-by-street small cells (het-nets) — the modern form of Q7's cell splitting.
  • Network slicing: many virtual slices of one physical network — an ultra-reliable slice for cars, a high-speed slice for video.
  • Edge computing (MEC): data centres next to towers — instant response for gaming/AR.
  • Massive IoT (mMTC): lakhs of sensors per cell (smart cities, agriculture) — tiny data, long battery.
  • Open RAN: open multi-vendor radio standards — operators deploy cheaper mix-and-match hardware.
  • AI-driven networks: traffic prediction, self-healing, energy saving — networks optimizing themselves.

Exam-ready plan: race opening line, 8 trend bullets, one example per bullet, link back to Q10 (5G row) at the end — the whole assignment reads as one story.
